Deep Dive: Agents on Both Sides of the Line — attackers, defenders and customer lines now run on software

Four stories from the last two days look unrelated and describe one shift. Anthropic's red team is studying whether small models can spread themselves. Semperis says most ransomware victims met AI-assisted attackers. Apate turns agents into decoy victims, and xAI says an agent already answers a large share of Starlink's support calls. In each, software with delegated authority is acting in an adversarial or customer-facing setting, and the control question is the same: who authorised it, and who can stop it?

Attackers are using the same agents. Semperis's 84% and 44% are survey claims from a security vendor, but they match the incentives: AI lowers the cost of reconnaissance and impersonation, and a company's own AI tools and connectors are credentialed paths into its systems. The practical consequence is that AI inventory and identity management belong in the same programme.

The defence bill is now visible. Bank of America's 1,000 extra contractors, hired to patch flaws exposed by frontier models such as Mythos, are the first hard number on what model-assisted vulnerability discovery costs a large company. Few enterprises have that budget, and the backlog does not wait for one.

Defence is becoming agent-versus-agent. Apate's decoys are a clear example: a cheap agent wastes a criminal's time and returns intelligence. The evidence so far is a vendor and one bank, and it shows a direction rather than a proven return. The buyers are the institutions that carry scam liability.

Customer lines are the largest live deployment. xAI's claim of about 20,000 Starlink calls a day, with roughly 70% resolved without a human, is the type of number executives are asked for. It is also the number least able to be checked from outside. Change control matters: the vendor moved live agents to the new model automatically.

The long tail is the unmanaged device. Anthropic's report is a reminder that capability can sit on hardware nobody counts. The year-end date the lab gives is a trend for matching its simplified-test capability, and the strongest test removed some security steps, but the control logic is clear: you cannot stop what you cannot inventory.
